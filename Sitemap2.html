EARTH & TIMBER

Inside actress Lucy Hale's restored 1930s Spanish colonial haven: an antidote to Hollywood velocity, sculpted by warm lime-plaster, reclaimed terracotta, and intuitive sanctuary design.

// TRANSMISSION 01: THE ARCHITECTURAL RESET

SERENITY PROTOCOL

Tucked within an enclave overlooking the Los Angeles basin sits a quiet rebuttal to the brassy glitter of the film industry. When actress Lucy Hale first walked onto the grounds of this 1930s residence, what she sought wasn’t an auditorium for entertaining or a pristine showroom of high-gloss opulence. It was an anchor.

Collaborating closely with designers Shannon Wollack and Brittany Zwickl of Studio Life.Style, Hale stripped back decades of disjointed alterations to expose the core authenticity of the home: weathered Saltillo tile floors, exposed hand-hewn ceiling beams, and lime-washed walls that breathe in the California warmth.

“Your home environment dictates the cadence of your nervous system,” Hale notes regarding the deliberate shift toward organic linens, unlacquered brass, and soft neutral earthen palettes. Alongside her rescue dogs Ethel and Elvis, the space operates less like a Hollywood property and more like a cloistered monastery—where reading corners, courtyard water fountains, and meditation niches replace vanity spaces.
